**Physical output meaning and validation.** `RawIntervalPartition.Raw` contains a natural record count, real left/right coordinates, record owners, and an owner of point 0. Its validity predicate has no valuation, fairness, success, or adversary parameter. `valid_iff_geometry` proves that validity is exactly boundedness of every used interval, pairwise disjointness of the `(left,right]` records, and their union being `(0,1]`. The records need not be sorted. Empty records are allowed, while zero records cannot cover the resource. There is no bound on the finite count across tapes, inputs, or executions, and no connection between output coordinates and queried coordinates.

The rational coverage test does not lose irrational points or the endpoint 1. If a point `x` in `(0,1]` were missing, choose a rational between `x` and the maximum of 0 and the finitely many right endpoints below `x`. A record covering that rational either also covers `x`, contradicting the missing-point assumption, or has a right endpoint below `x`, contradicting maximality. The proof uses finiteness and the right-closed convention exactly where required. It is not a density-of-rationals argument incorrectly applied to an arbitrary union.

The raw decoder constructs the actual `IntervalAllocation.Allocation`. `decoded_share` preserves each share as a set, not merely its total length or a surrogate utility matrix; `decoded_value` consequently preserves the signed-density measure value for every integrable row. `decode_ofAllocation` and `ofAllocation_measurable` give an exact measurable round trip for every previously admitted canonical allocation, so the new encoding does not impose a fragmentation or record-order restriction on that model.

The boundary convention assigns `(left,right]` to the record owner and explicitly assigns 0. Thus every decoded allocation is literally complete and pairwise disjoint. `value_eq_of_endpoint_change` proves that any measurable share agreeing away from the finite set of record endpoints has the same actual signed-density value. This applies to the nonnegative hard rows as well. The corresponding mixed values are the density integrals restricted to the resource, so the physical interpretation is unchanged. The endpoint theorem supplies the mathematical null-boundary equivalence; it does not itself construct a new endpoint-tag syntax or establish a compiler from every such syntax.

`RawPartition` is the older, narrower adjacent-cut representation: nondecreasing cuts start at 0 and end at 1. Its geometric and measurable decoder is sound. The final `NonnegativeRawMain` and `MixedIntervalMain` endpoints use `RawIntervalPartition`, so the sorted adjacent-cut restriction is absent from those endpoints.

**Success and actual query costs.** `OutputPostprocess.run_normalized` is an equality between actual recursively executed runs. It preserves the entire answered list and transforms only the stop. A successful decode yields the decoded return; an unsuccessful decode yields query-free divergence; existing divergence, illegal calls, and exhausted caps retain their respective stop constructors. This is input-independent geometric validation, without an extra oracle request or a fairness-dependent repair.

`returned_normalized_iff` gives both directions of the finite-return correspondence. `NonnegativeRawMain.normalized_success` and `MixedIntervalMain.normalized_success` then identify the full eventual success events. Their raw success predicates require both valid geometry and the literal physical fairness comparisons. In particular, wrong valid returns remain failures, and an invalid raw return cannot later become successful by increasing the fuel cap. The nonnegative module proves the latter explicitly as `malformed_return_failure`; the same terminal-stop simulation underlies the mixed adapter.

`AttemptedQueries.attempts` is the answered-list length plus one exactly for a terminal illegal request. An unconsulted request at an exhausted cap contributes no extra call. Taking the supremum over caps gives the intended complete attempted-call count: a terminal illegal call after `q` answers costs `q+1`; other terminal behaviors cost their answered count; query-free nonreturn may have finite cost; endless querying has infinite cost. `attemptedCost_of_terminal` proves stabilization at an actual terminal behavior. The lower bound charges all tapes and is not conditional on success or termination.

Both `queryCost_normalized` and `attemptedCost_normalized` are pointwise equalities for arbitrary oracles, tapes, and initial histories. Thus validation preserves infinite costs and finite-cost failures as well as successful runs. The expectation transports use those equalities. `NonnegativeRawMain.main_attemptedCost_eq` also identifies the earlier determining-request convention with this shared attempted-call definition. Passing from answered-cost lower bounds to attempted-cost lower bounds uses the correct pointwise domination direction and incurs no extra additive loss.

**Finite-step invariant through both captures.** `FiniteStepDensity.FiniteStep` gives an actual finite breakpoint set and requires the density to be constant on each cell determined by comparisons with those breakpoints. Requiring both strict and non-strict comparisons permits separate singleton boundary cells, which is harmless for atomless values. This is a finite piecewise-constant certificate, not merely a finite-range assertion. The closure lemmas explicitly combine finite breakpoint sets for sums and restrictions, and reflect them by `b ↦ 1-b`.

The initial state is the unit constant cell. `MixedFiniteStep.advance_allStep` proves that an update preserves the certificate: the chosen row is unchanged, and every other row is an old prefix plus two constant cells. Both `MixedExecution.Extends` and `RightmostExecution.Extends` carry the preservation implication `AllStep S → AllStep T`. That field is discharged by `advance_allStep` in `advance_extends`, by identity in reflexivity, and by composition in transitivity. It is not a supplied hardness premise.

I traced its use through the concrete construction. Both `exists_query_capture` and `exists_output_capture` construct their next state with `advance_extends`. Finite trace capture, batch composition, and finite amplification compose those witnessed extensions. `MixedFiniteStepMain.left_finite_horizon` and `right_finite_horizon` apply the final extension's `steps` field to `initial_allStep`, obtaining finite-step certificates for the actual hard states selected by the probability argument. There is no infinite limiting profile. The rightmost physical input additionally uses the proved `reflectRow_finiteStep`, so reflection does not silently leave the subclass.
