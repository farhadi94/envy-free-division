**Mathematical consistency and dependency checks**

For deterministic chores, the main theorem is supported by Appendix A and the fully supplied prerequisites in Appendices B–E. The important distinctions are maintained: a recipient is not automatically the observer whose comparison needs compensation; a suffix's full cost is not its net own credit; an all-zero clipped proposal cannot be used as a physical affine donor. The ordinary advancing operation uses clipped utilities and strips zero-own proposals, while the atomic and terminal closed operations use the stronger affine contract. The latter requires nonnegative whole-parent utilities and therefore really retains its whole physical parent even at zero own utility. I found no use of a general exact quota oracle in the completion argument: its splitter only supplies lower shares, which are exactly what the two-group proof uses.

The masked pooling call has `d` distinct actual observers and `d+1` roots. Its many-parent SubCore precondition therefore holds even for `d=n`, and its endpoint recording needs one enclosing-observer factor rather than an additional task factor. The Core's hungry-profile argument is used as an existence witness for the finite persistent-record program; the physical algorithm is not querying the auxiliary hungry measure. The schedule counts at most `n-1` mergers and `2n-1` revelations. Its floor-aware pool bound, together with the `4^n` Core cost and polynomial clearing/recording overhead, supports the advertised leading exponent `3n log_2 n`. The looser explicit exponent `14n(ceil(log_2 n)+1)` is consistent with, rather than the derivation of, that sharper asymptotic bound.

For the randomized upper bound, I traced the interpolation estimate, aggregate endpoint charge, maximum-cell envelope, assignment isolation, numerical credits/taxes, physical correction, Snapshot, shrinking, and global completion. The two preference directions use different edge orientations and optimization directions, but both produce the stated margin inequality. The reserve is fixed by independent labels for the interpolation analysis; the later acceptance events are charged as exceptional events rather than used to assert an unjustified conditional sampling law. Corrections include the first rejected correction, and Snapshot's alternatives keep the original interpolation table. The circular inclusion argument explicitly handles mass charged by foreign measures on cutter-null regions. Nonanticipating arbitrary roots do not supply hidden future information.

The global upper-bound proof preserves frozen-agent guards in the correct direction: frozen cake agents are protected against additions to active bundles; active chore agents are protected against the cost they may subsequently receive relative to frozen bundles. Whole-bundle permutations transport old domination targets, and positive residue prevents a proper target from silently becoming the observer itself. The potential is compared at the saved committed allocation, which is necessary because temporary additive stages may already have larger domination counts. With `m=O(n^18 log^2(2n))`, `O(n^2)` committed iterations and `O(n)` recording cost per sampled endpoint give the claimed expected-query and expected-Cut exponents, respectively. No bit-complexity conclusion follows, and none is claimed.

For the nonnegative lower bound, the statement requires success on the entire normalized finite-step class even though expected cost is measured at the uniform profile. That distinction prevents the superficial zero-query objection based on returning equal lengths on the uniform input. The proof preserves determining intervals for all finite-query outcomes, includes the possible terminal illegal request, and retains old failure mass before adding newly spoiled successful runs. This is the component that permits arbitrary fixed positive success probability. The common pulse width is chosen from the uniform execution law before sampling the perturbed input and tape. Finitely many output boundaries on each returned allocation suffice for the limiting argument; no expectation bound on their number is needed. The optimized scalar bound and its condition `np >= 3-2/n` agree with the main text. The proof concerns exact EF and does not imply a fixed positive additive-tolerance lower bound on this bounded-density class.

For the mixed result, the signed relative definition is meaningful at both negative and zero utilities. Directly, for `y>=0` its comparison is equivalent to `x>=alpha*y`; for `y<0` it is equivalent to `x>=y/alpha`. It contains exact EF and gives the usual relaxed chore inequality after setting `v=-c`. Positive row total forces a positive own bundle under relative EF, so the reduction to the ordinary cake inequality on the net-one hard profiles is valid. The combined inequality with `0<=epsilon<alpha<=1` is used only to prove the two advertised approximation cases; it is not presented as a general signed-relative-plus-additive definition.

I also checked the mixed proof's quantifier order. Protected query histories are constructed before covering a high-probability family of finite outputs; the output-size quantile is not used to choose the old query-interval variation budget. Variation-weighted discrepancy sampling cancels the potentially large number of pulses in the replay-loss estimate. The last separated pure pair forces failure even when the base output was already incorrect; if no pair separates, the bundle-value table is unchanged. The explicit measurable upper indicator permits selection of one fixed profile and one fixed Borel selector independently of the realized tape. The guarded finite-horizon simulator uses fixed tuple-based selectors, so its extra legality Evals do not change the simulated roots. The final conclusion is an unbounded supremum of expected query counts, not one input of infinite expected cost. The absence of a common total-variation ceiling and the lack of any prescribed first/last-root policy are substantive assumptions and are stated in the main theorem.
