**Lemma H.6: an explicit measurable induction.** References are to `sections/mixed_lower_proof.tex`, principally `r6m:query-protection`, lines 277–340. At the start of stage (k), fix the current profile/selector pair and a measurable retained set (G). For every retained tape, the first (k-1) calls that exist have their argument endpoints and selected roots in the protected intervals or at 0 and 1. Those replies survive the permitted subsequent modifications. If fewer calls exist, the return, abort, or continued internal nontermination after that prefix survives as well.

Define, under this fixed stage-start pair,

\[
 E_{k,i}=G\cap\{\text{the }k\text{th call exists and addresses row }i\},
 \qquad i\in[n].
\]

Each group is measurable by the fixed-input assumptions in H.1. The groups are disjoint. They partition the retained tapes on which a (k)th call exists; the other tapes need no new coordinates. Their definitions depend on the tape and the preceding (k-1) replies, not on the (k)th reply. Consequently, the groups' memberships on surviving tapes are unchanged as the rows are processed. A continuum of possible transcripts is permitted: the construction partitions by only the finite row label, not by individual transcript values.

For row (i), intersect this fixed group with the currently surviving set. Under the **then-current fixed pair**, take its measurable endpoint/root maps (f_\ell). There are at most three. They can be extended by the constant 0 when a coordinate does not exist; 0 is outside the covering domain. For Borel (B\subset U=(0,1)\setminus\bigcup_sJ_s), the measure used in the proof is explicitly

\[
 \mu(B)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}
 \mathbb P\{\omega\in G_{\rm current}\cap E_{k,i}:f_\ell(\omega)\in B\}.
\]

This is a finite nonnegative Borel measure of total mass at most three. It requires neither a regular conditional distribution given a transcript nor joint measurability in an abstract space of profiles. The covering lemma produces a deterministic finite collection (C) of intervals with \(\mu(U\setminus C)<\beta/(nh)\). The discarded tape set is a finite union of measurable inverse images, and its original probability is at most this quantity. No conditional renormalization occurs.

Insertion leaves the entire addressed row (i) unchanged and preserves every old cumulative function on the old protected intervals. Thus:

- Earlier calls replay in every row, including both bracket legality and the old root equation.
- The current row-(i) call and reply replay because that whole row is unchanged; its coordinates become protected.
- An unprocessed row group has the same preceding (k-1) replies and hence the same next query tuple. Its next root may have changed. That causes no problem because its root law is measured only when its group is processed.

This is the induction's crucial distinction. Computing all row groups' root laws at the beginning of a stage would be unjustified; the manuscript instead explicitly uses the current pair at each group. After all (n) groups, every surviving (k)th reply is protected. The next stage can therefore begin under one fixed new pair. There are only (nh) updates, with total discarded probability below \(\beta\). Every retained set is measurable, and every input/selector selected during this construction is deterministic data chosen from the strategy's law rather than from the realized tape.

The local variation ceiling also survives: every insertion preserves the restriction of each signed measure to every reserved interval. Total variation restricted to such an interval is determined by that restricted signed measure, so its sum-variation budget stays unchanged. The proof does not confuse unchanged interval restrictions with unchanged cumulative levels; the latter are required for old intervals, while global preservation of the currently queried row handles the newly reserved ones.

**Selector patch: domain precision and Borelness.** The corrected `r6m:selector` statement, lines 124–151, now requires that the query be legal for both profiles and that the old selected point satisfy the new root equation. The explicit legality clause is justified by this normalized example. Let the old density be one. Query ([a,b]=[0,1/2]), target (t=1/4), with old root (x=1/4). Let the new density be

\[
 f'(u)=\begin{cases}
 1,&0\le u\le1/4,\\
 -3/5,&1/4<u\le1/2,\\
 9/5,&1/2<u\le1.
 \end{cases}
\]

It still has whole value one, and (F'(1/4)=1/4), so the old root equation remains true. However, (F'(1/2)=1/10), and the target (1/4) is outside the new legal bracket range. Mere preservation of a root equation does not ensure that a tuple lies in the new selector's legal domain. This was a precision issue in the lemma's wording; its proof already begins on queries legal for the new profile.

Every use meets the repaired condition. In H.6, prior queries preserve both endpoint cumulatives and the selected root cumulative; the current group's whole queried row is unchanged. Output-interval installation preserves all old query coordinates. In final pulse replay, every pair's total-zero corridor avoids every occupied old interval, so the same endpoint and root cumulatives survive. Thus none of these uses relies on the invalid implication illustrated above.

For Borelness, the legal domain is Borel because (F) is continuous. The fallback least root exists by the intermediate value theorem and compactness. For a rational cutoff (r), existence of a root in ([a,\min(b,r)]) is a Borel condition: the interval must be nonempty, and the minimum of the continuous residual over it must equal zero. The change of variable to a fixed compact unit interval makes that minimum continuous in the parameters on its domain. Taking countable unions over rational cutoffs characterizes strict sublevel sets of the least-root map. Old/new legality, bracket membership, and the new root equation are Borel tests, so patching preserves Borelness. This remains valid for zero targets, zero-length brackets, flat cumulative regions, and repeated query tuples.

**Finite geometric construction and product measurability.** The cover proof works even for atomic or singular coordinate distributions. The mesh cut points avoid the countably many atoms of the finite coordinate law, allowing neighborhoods of those cut points to have arbitrarily small total lost mass. Atomlessness and uniform continuity of the variation cumulative permit the required small variation in each retained interval. No finite-support probability law for the algorithm tape is assumed; only the number of installed intervals and physical pulse supports is finite.

After fixing the query-protected pair, the output-count quantile exists because each return has a finite encoding. It does not require a finite expected boundary count. Canonicalization, the number of genuine boundaries, and the ordered boundary coordinates are measurable after stratifying the finite encodings by their lengths. The retained set after the boundary cover is therefore measurable. The newly installed intervals and final anchors are fixed before pulse sampling and before any realized algorithm tape is used.

For the base pair, let (\lambda=\nu|_O/V). When (V<c), the proof ends without sampling. Otherwise (V\ge c>0), (L<\infty), and (\Xi\) has the finite product law \(\lambda^L\), independent of the tape. Removing the finitely many interval endpoints and representatives changes none of this probability. Each support radius is a Borel minimum of positive geometric distances and the fixed width \(\rho\), so every realized support is nondegenerate. A sample arbitrarily near an excluded endpoint may produce a very narrow support, but its density is still finite for that individual sample. Finitely many such pulses preserve finite variation and piecewise constancy. Neither a common density bound nor a uniform positive width is needed.

Canonical ownership (A(\omega,x)) is jointly measurable in the base tape and spatial coordinate: on each finite encoding stratum, it is determined by finitely many comparisons with measurable endpoints and discrete owner labels. A support's impurity is likewise a finite endpoint-comparison event. For each fixed finite returned output, the atomless sample law misses every genuine boundary almost surely. Along a deterministic sequence \(\rho_m\downarrow0\), all its positive supports eventually become pure. Tonelli and bounded convergence on the product of the fixed tape law and sample law therefore give one deterministic width with the claimed small product probability of impurity. Width is not selected separately for individual tapes. Negative supports are already pure because their fixed gaps contain no retained output boundary.

**The explicit majorant avoids a joint-profile measurability gap.** In `r6m:selection`, the event (R\) is measurable: for the finite old interval list, occupancy is a finite union of coordinate-in-interval events, and sample avoidance is a finite intersection. Base-output correctness is measurable because the fixed base profile's interval values are continuous functions of endpoints; the finite sums and (n^2) comparisons remain measurable on each encoding stratum. Thus every summand in the displayed (Z(\omega,\xi)\) is measurable on the ordinary product space.

For each fixed sample, the patched selector is a single Borel function of the query tuple, with no tape dependence. On (H\cap R\), all old oracle replies replay. A base nonreturn stays a nonreturn. For a finite pure return, the last separated pulse forces error; if no pair separates, every bundle value stays unchanged. Therefore new success implies either one of the three exceptional events or a **correct base output** with every sampled pair coassigned. In particular, initially incorrect outputs are not silently omitted from the success bound.

The pointwise domination by (Z\) holds for every sample in the specified sample space and every tape. If a sampled point happens to be an output boundary, its symmetric support is impure and the exceptional event covers it. One need not make the actual perturbed-success event jointly measurable over sample and tape: its section is measurable for each fixed input/selector, and the measurable (Z\) bounds each section pointwise. Tonelli applies to (Z\), whose mean is at most (3\delta/4), and selects one fixed sample with section integral at most \(\delta\). No measurable choice of the entire family of selector patches, and no probability measure on an abstract profile space, is being assumed.

**Quantifier order and truncation.** The choices occur in this order: fix the whole strategy, tape law, horizon, and tolerances; choose the old variation budget; construct finitely many query covers and selector patches; fix the boundary quantile and output cover; fix the base pair and anchors; choose the finite independent pulse-sampling law; choose a deterministic width and mass sequence; and finally use integration to choose one sample. The resulting profile and selector can depend on the strategy's law but never on its realized tape. The sampling law is never conditioned on the realized query transcript. For expected cost, the selected pair may change with the cutoff (q); the conclusion is an infinite supremum of expected costs, not that one input must have infinite expectation.

The revised guard in the final proof explicitly checks malformed syntax, row validity, and (0\le a\le b\le1) **before** any additional Eval. This makes its claim of pointwise legality immediate even when the original algorithm issues, on a failure tape, a tuple such as \(\operatorname{Cut}(i,-1/10,1/2,0)\). After these free checks, the extra Eval safely reveals the bracket total and the target-range test decides whether to issue the Cut. The original wording could intend these geometric checks within “malformed syntax”; making them explicit eliminates an ordering ambiguity.

At most (2q) actual calls are made. The extra replies are hidden from the simulated procedure, and the fixed tuple-based selector is unaffected by their occurrence. The new success event is exactly original legal success with attempted-call count at most (q). Stopping on illegal requests discards only failures. Internal nontermination remains permitted and does not require deciding whether the original program will halt. Applying finite-horizon hardness gives \(\mathbb P(\mathcal S,Q>q)\ge p-\delta\), hence \(\mathbb E Q\ge(q+1)(p-\delta)\). A history-sensitive root policy would invalidate the straightforward hidden-Eval simulation; it is outside the stated selector model.
